## Findings

### F1. The recap is a retelling no one checked against you

Claude Code writes the "recap:" when you return. It is written by the same model that did the work,
about its own work. That is self-witnessing. Wilson (p. 71) tells of retelling Cora's story at a
conference. She felt he had misrepresented parts of it, so in the book he uses her published
version, which she could check against what she meant. A recap is a retelling that the person it is
for never checked. That is why the witness seat reads the peer session from outside and why your
words go into the circle verbatim. Also, the recap came out in the 🧠 and 🌸 voices with no plugin
involved. That bears on your 2026-10-02 question about whether a persona exists only when a plugin
is loaded. Here the voice came from the instruction files that every call loads, including platform
features.

### F2. Read as provenance, the screen has two of the five links the Miadi Factory needs

Our `knowledge-organization-archives-and-provenance` foundation applies W3C PROV-DM:

- The status line is a record of an activity: 5 m 54 s, ended at 9:08.
- The shell panel is an activity still running: the listener that belongs to the seat.
- The recap is an entity derived from the session with no attribution. Nobody signs it.

The Factory intention links five things: the originating question, the session trace, the interface
evidence, the explicit criteria, and a person's judgment. The screen holds the trace and the
interface evidence. It has no originating question and no criteria. Your judgment arrived only now,
as "boring, passive". The same foundation cites respect des fonds (Jenkinson 1922, Schellenberg
1956): each session keeps its own records. Your pane note confirms this, and I measured it. Pane 1
(`%108`) holds the listener and the mission. Pane 2 (`%109`, this fork) inherited the transcript but
not the process. So when this fork was told the listener had "stopped", that was a break in the
chain of custody (Bearman 1994). The listener itself never stopped.

### F3. The field the recap belongs to is one our foundations do not hold

Interruption and resumption research in HCI studies how the cue a person meets on returning decides
how fast they recover the goal they left. Altmann & Trafton (2002) model this as memory for goals,
and Trafton et al. (2003) study how people prepare to resume. I cite both from memory, not from a
reading done in this turn. Our HCI foundation holds Shneiderman's "overview first" and progressive
disclosure, but not this field. The consequence: the recap is the cue you meet when you come back,
and nothing records it or checks it. I recommend making the cue you meet on returning the attributed
one: the inventory record and the circle, signed by the seat, with your words verbatim.
